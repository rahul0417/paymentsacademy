Proposed Slide Structure: "Anatomy of a Swipe"

Each box represents one slide in the micro-learning deck. Click the approach that feels right.

A

Detailed Flow (8-10 slides)

1. What You'll Learn — overview of the module + learning objectives
2. The Big Picture — "What happens when you tap your card?" simple narrative
3. The Players — the 5 parties + 2 processors explained simply
4. Act 1: Authorization — real-time approval flow with sequence diagram
5. Act 2: Clearing — end-of-day batching explained with diagram
6. Act 3: Settlement — money movement + fee breakdown visual
7. Key Terms — glossary cards for new terms (BIN, ISO 8583, interchange, etc.)
8. Quick Quiz — 4 multiple-choice questions embedded inline

Pros

  • Each concept gets breathing room
  • Diagrams don't feel rushed
  • Natural pacing

Cons

  • More slides to click through
  • Some topics might need 12+ slides
B

Compact Flow (5-6 slides)

1. What You'll Learn — overview + learning objectives
2. The Concept — full explanation in 1-2 dense slides with inline diagrams
3. Visual Walkthrough — sequence diagram + flow diagram on one slide
4. Key Terms — glossary definitions
5. Quick Quiz — questions

Pros

  • Faster to complete
  • Consistent slide count across topics

Cons

  • Slides may feel dense
  • Diagrams compete with text for space
C

Adaptive Flow (varies per topic)

1. What You'll Learn — always present
2-N. Concept slides — as many as needed, one idea per slide, diagrams get their own slide
N+1. Key Terms — always present
N+2. Quick Quiz — always present

Simple topics get 5 slides. Complex topics like cross-border payments might get 10+. Each slide is focused — never more than one concept or one diagram per slide.

Pros

  • Each slide is digestible
  • Diagrams get full attention
  • Truly "micro" — one idea at a time

Cons

  • Slide count varies (users can't predict length)
  • More authoring effort per topic
